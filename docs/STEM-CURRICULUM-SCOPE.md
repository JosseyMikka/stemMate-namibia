# STEM curriculum scope and sources

Catalogue reviewed against official public listings on 16 October 2026. This is a
routing catalogue plus original starter activities, not certification of syllabus
alignment. Schools must check current editions and review activities before assessed
use. School grade is distinct from Cambridge qualification.

The app accepts examination years 2026/2027 only. IGCSE science and Computing
entries use the 2026–2028 syllabus window. Advanced sciences use 2025–2027.
Advanced Computing separates the 2026 edition from 2027–2029: a class cannot use
the 2026 course ID for a 2027 exam. Mathematics retains its existing edition windows.
Namibia entries point to the official subject listing and explicitly ask the school
to confirm the current edition; some NIED PDFs were unavailable during verification.

## Available activities

- Mathematics: linear equations, proportion, Pythagoras, quadratics, differentiation,
  integration, restricted by qualification.
- Physics: average speed/data limitations; resultant forces, acceleration and velocity.
- Chemistry: atom conservation and balancing; amount of substance and molar ratios.
- Biology: osmosis evidence/percentage changes; enzymes, controls and uncertainty.
- Computing: trace/debug inclusive versus exclusive loops; binary search invariants.

The eight new activities are shared concept/prerequisite revision across the listed
courses. They do not establish full Core/Extended or AS/A Level coverage, and do not
assess practical competence. More advanced topics and teacher-reviewed progression
remain work for subsequent content releases. All questions are original exercises,
not reproductions of official papers. Each has three progressive hints, a model
explanation, changed-context application and an independent check.

## Safeguards and evaluation

Science activities require no substances, apparatus, living samples or personal
health data. Displayed notices and tutor prompts prohibit practical instructions
and direct learners to supervised, approved school work. Computing never executes
code and uses fictional data; prompts prohibit credential collection, exploitation
and unsafe commands. These prompt constraints cannot guarantee safe AI output;
live-provider evaluation is still required. Starter lessons avoid provider calls.

A teacher can own classes across all subjects and both pathways. Class policies,
memberships and activity totals stay separate; the course ID determines subject
on the server. A learner cannot substitute another subject's topic or bypass class
membership. The same guided stage gates and independent-help restrictions apply
across subjects. Counts are process totals, not cross-subject learning scores.
Research should evaluate subject-specific reasoning and delayed unaided transfer.
