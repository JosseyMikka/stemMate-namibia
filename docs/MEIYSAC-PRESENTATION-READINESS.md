# STEMMate: MEIYSAC presentation and pilot readiness

Review date: 18 October 2026. Prepared for discussion with the Ministry of Education, Innovation, Youth, Sports, Arts and Culture (MEIYSAC). This is a technical and policy briefing, not a legal opinion, Ministry endorsement or an application already submitted.

## The decision to request

Request a small, supervised, time-limited feasibility pilot and the review needed to authorise it. Do not request national adoption on the strength of a software demonstration. The Ministry is not obliged to fund or approve this project simply because it is presented.

STEMMate is a STEM thinking companion for Grades 10–12. Learners can ask questions independently; teachers can prepare materials by selecting subject, grade, curriculum/qualification and examination year. Neither activity requires creating a class or adding a roster. Optional class spaces support teacher-directed projects and feedback. Accounts remain required.

## What to ask MEIYSAC for

1. A named coordination contact and introductions to curriculum/NIED, regional education, ICT/security, inclusion, research and procurement teams.
2. Subject reviewers for Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Computing/Computer Science, and confirmation of the correct NSSCO/NSSCAS versions and objective mappings. Start the pilot with a deliberately small reviewed topic set.
3. Written decisions on pilot scope, participating schools, access route, duration, teacher supervision and training time. Agree device/connectivity needs and a fair alternative for learners unable to participate.
4. Written data and safeguarding conditions: approved host and AI provider, permitted data, processing locations, retention/deletion, incident contacts, research consent and withdrawal. Do not upload identifiable learner work for a demonstration.
5. An agreed evaluation plan and reporting audience, including whether the activity is research requiring additional approvals.
6. A separate discussion of sponsorship or procurement after scope, evidence, supplier arrangements and costs are clear. Seek guidance rather than implying the Ministry owes funds, devices or student records.

## What STEMMate must provide

Provide a short protocol, demo with fictional data, honest feature/coverage statement, subject mapping samples, a data-flow and retention inventory, provider terms and processing controls for review, safeguarding and incident procedures, named operational responsibility, teacher training plan, accessibility testing results, hosting/backup/restore plan, and a costed bounded pilot proposal. Complete supplier registration and agree collaboration/commercial rights before contracting. No blanket compliance or accreditation claim is made by this review.

The research should test whether learners can solve a fresh task without AI, including a delayed check, not merely whether they clicked hints or submitted attractive work. Compare supported performance with independent performance; consider a suitable comparison condition, teacher workload, participation equity and a subject-expert rubric. Agree analysis, sample size and ethics with supervisors. Bloom-labelled activities alone do not prove higher-order thinking; a single making activity does not establish project-based learning or constructionist learning outcomes.

## Current software discrepancies and limits

- Course IDs, grades, exam years and authored topic membership are validated. Biology and Computing have separate catalogues. These controls do not guarantee the factual accuracy or subject fit of arbitrary AI output.
- A live preview contained a Computing loops worksheet under Mathematics. A new required AI context-status check rejects detected mismatches with a clarification message, and invalid/missing status cannot become a draft. This is an AI check, not deterministic semantic verification. Teachers must still review subject/objective fit.
- A stalled teacher-preparation browser request previously had no deadline. It now releases after 100 seconds, preserves inputs and any prior draft, and never automatically retries. A server request may still finish and consume allowance after browser cancellation.
- AI materials can still contain wrong science, leaked answers, weak independent questions, inaccurate timings or unjustified labels. Editable drafts and review reminders reduce harm but do not solve factual validation.
- Only 14 authored starters and selected diagrams/conceptual simulations exist. The catalogue does not mean full syllabus coverage. Paper uploads support formative assistance after transcription confirmation, not official examination marking or verified mark schemes.
- Ordinary guided chat uses a structured reflection flow; it does not automatically assess every learner explanation. No proven learning-gain claim is justified yet.
- Local demo previews are not production hosting. Production security/provider approval, physical-device and assistive-technology testing, classroom load testing, expert content validation and incident readiness remain rollout gates. In-memory learning sessions/limits require a single server instance.
- Cleaned selected repository history does not prove deletion of historical copies in upstream forks, caches, old clones or backups. Resolve the residual exposure assessment and rights/retention obligations separately before real learner use.

## Billing: deliberately open

Owner decision: leave billing unconfigured while the supplier/business is being set up. The app has no checkout, invoicing, subscription enforcement or billing-data collection. Do not put bank information, tax documents or unapproved prices in the learner interface or AI prompts. No software change to collect billing details is needed for this presentation.

Before a paid pilot: establish the legal supplier, public business contact, applicable tax/invoice obligations with qualified advice, cost model, approved pricing, contract/usage limits, support obligations, cancellation and refund terms, authorised institutional purchaser and procurement process. Keep institutional billing in a restricted business/admin area separate from learners and teaching data. Decide whether an external accounting service is more appropriate before building invoicing. A future quote or invoice needs the appropriate organisational details and references; it must not imply MEIYSAC approval merely because its name appears.

A reasonable commercial hypothesis is a bounded school/institution pilot with agreed support and training, potentially sponsored for learners. Prices remain to be validated against AI, hosting, content review, support and accessibility costs. Do not promise unlimited AI use, guaranteed outcomes or sell learner data.
